S10 · ANOVA: One-Way and Two-Way

Why this matters for the term final. ANOVA is a big-mark question that appears almost every year. 2021-22 Q8(b) (18 marks): one-way ANOVA with unequal group sizes (three salesmen) — solved below. 2022-23 Q8(b) (10 marks): one-way ANOVA for four brands of bulbs at 5% and 1%, and Q8(c) (15 marks): two-way ANOVA (corn types × soil blocks). 2023-24 Q8(b) (20 marks): one-way ANOVA for prices in three cities. The 2022-23 and 2023-24 solutions are in TF 2022-23 Solutions and TF 2023-24 Solutions; this note teaches the method and includes a fresh two-way practice. The calculation is completely mechanical: learn the five formulas and the table layout and you cannot lose these marks.

1. The idea of analysis of variance

Suppose we want to compare the means of three or more groups (three teaching methods, four bulb brands, three cities’ prices). Doing many separate two-sample t-tests would be tedious and would inflate the chance of a Type I error. Analysis of variance (ANOVA), developed by R. A. Fisher, tests all the means at once:

H0​:μ1​=μ2​=⋯=μk​againstH1​:at least two means are unequal

Memorize: The ANOVA idea

==ANOVA splits the total variation of all observations into parts due to identifiable sources (variation between groups, caused by differences among group means) and a part due to chance (variation within groups).== If the between-group variation is large compared with the within-group variation, the group means are significantly different. The comparison is made with the F ratio: F=mean square within groups (error)mean square between groups​ If H0​ is true, both mean squares estimate the same σ2 and F is close to 1. A large F leads to rejection. The test is always right-tailed.

Memorize: Assumptions of ANOVA

The samples are drawn independently and at random; each population is normally distributed; all populations have the same variance σ2 (homogeneity of variance); and the effects are additive.

2. F-test for two variances (brief)

To test H0​:σ12​=σ22​, compute F=s22​s12​​ with the larger sample variance in the numerator (so F≥1), and compare with Fα,(n1​−1),(n2​−1)​ (for a two-tailed test use α/2 in the table). Lecture example: route variances 8.99472 and 4.37532 give F=4.23; with 6 and 7 df at α/2=0.05, the critical value is 3.87, so the variances differ. This is the same F distribution that ANOVA uses (see S7 Sampling Distributions CLT and Confidence Intervals).

3. One-way ANOVA (completely randomized design)

We have k groups; group i has ni​ observations with total Ti​. Let N=∑ni​ and G=∑Ti​ (grand total).

Memorize: One-way ANOVA formulas

Correction term: CT=NG2​

Total SS: ST​=∑∑xij2​−CT

Between-groups SS: SB​=∑i=1k​ni​Ti2​​−CT

Within-groups (error) SS: SW​=ST​−SB​

F=SW​/(N−k)SB​/(k−1)​∼F(k−1),(N−k)​ Reject H0​ if Fcal​>Fα,(k−1),(N−k)​.

Memorize: One-way ANOVA table

Why it works. ST​ measures how all N observations vary around the grand mean. SB​ measures how the group means vary around the grand mean (weighted by group size). SW​ is what is left: the scatter of observations around their own group mean, which can only be chance. Note that the formula ∑Ti2​/ni​ handles unequal group sizes automatically; just divide each squared total by its own ni​.

Exam trick: coding the data

Subtracting the same constant from every observation does not change ST​, SB​, SW​ or F, and dividing every observation by the same constant c divides every sum of squares by c2, so F is unchanged. So you may subtract a convenient number (e.g. 20) or divide by 100 to make the arithmetic small. State clearly that you coded the data. If you report sums of squares, convert back (multiply by c2).

4. Two-way ANOVA without replication (randomized block design)

Sometimes the observations are classified by two factors, with exactly one observation in each cell. A typical example: four types of corn (treatments, columns) are each planted in five blocks of land (rows), and the blocks may differ in soil fertility. Two-way ANOVA separates the variation due to rows, the variation due to columns, and the remaining random error, and tests both factors.

Let there be r rows and c columns, N=rc observations, row totals Ri​, column totals Cj​, grand total G.

Common mistakes in ANOVA

Forgetting to subtract CT; dividing row totals by the number of rows instead of the number of columns; using N instead of N−k for the error df; putting the smaller mean square in the numerator; and, with coded data, forgetting that F is unchanged but sums of squares scale by c2. When a question asks for two significance levels (5% and 1%), compare the same Fcal​ with both critical values and write both decisions.

5. How the parts connect

ST​=SB​+SW​ (one-way) and ST​=SR​+SC​+SE​ (two-way) mirror the regression identity SST=SSR+SSE in S6 Correlation and Regression: in every case we split total variation into an explained part and an unexplained part, and the F ratio compares them after dividing each by its degrees of freedom. The degrees of freedom also add up: (k−1)+(N−k)=N−1.

Self-check

1. Three groups of sizes 4, 5, 6. Degrees of freedom of F? k−1=2 and N−k=15−3=12.

2. G=120, N=12, ∑x2=1300. Find CT and ST​. CT=14400/12=1200; ST​=100.

3. In a two-way table with 5 rows and 4 columns, what are the error df? (5−1)(4−1)=12.

4. MSB​=12, MSW​=3, critical F=3.89. Decision? F=4>3.89: reject H0​, means differ.

5. Why can’t we just do several t-tests instead of ANOVA? Many separate tests increase the overall chance of a Type I error and are tedious; ANOVA tests all means simultaneously at the chosen α.
