S10 · ANOVA: One-Way and Two-Way

Why this matters for the TF. ANOVA appeared in 2021-22 Q8(b) (three salesmen, unequal sizes), 2022-23 Q8(b) (one-way, four bulb brands) and Q8(c) (two-way, soil blocks × corn types), and 2023-24 Q8(b) (one-way, prices in three cities, 20 marks).

The calculation is long but purely mechanical, so a practised student collects full marks. The 2022-23 and 2023-24 questions are solved in TF 2022-23 Solutions and TF 2023-24 Solutions.

Core tools (memorize)

Memorize: one-way ANOVA ( k groups, N observations in total)

G is the grand total, Ti​ the total of group i and ni​ its size.

Correction term:

CT=NG2​

Total sum of squares:

ST​=∑∑x2−CT

Between-groups sum of squares:

SB​=∑i​ni​Ti2​​−CT

Within-groups (error) sum of squares:

SW​=ST​−SB​

F ratio:

F=SW​/(N−k)SB​/(k−1)​∼Fk−1, N−k​

==Reject H0​ (all means equal) if Fcal​>Fα​(k−1,N−k).==

Memorize: two-way ANOVA ( r blocks/rows × c treatments/columns, one value per cell)

Correction term:

CT=rcG2​

Total sum of squares:

ST​=∑x2−CT

Rows (blocks) sum of squares:

SR​=c∑Ri2​​−CT

Columns (treatments) sum of squares:

SC​=r∑Cj2​​−CT

Error sum of squares:

SE​=ST​−SR​−SC​

F ratio for rows:

FR​=SE​/[(r−1)(c−1)]SR​/(r−1)​

F ratio for columns:

FC​=SE​/[(r−1)(c−1)]SC​/(c−1)​

Memorize: ANOVA table layout (always draw it)

SourcedfSSMS = SS/dfF
Between groups (or rows / columns)k−1SB​MSB​MSB​/MSW​
Within (error)N−kSW​MSW​
TotalN−1ST​

Minimal theory

ANOVA tests H0​:μ1​=μ2​=⋯=μk​ against H1​: at least two means differ.

It assumes independent random samples from normal populations with a common variance. The test is always right-tailed.

Coding trick

Subtracting a constant from every value changes nothing.

Dividing every value by h divides every SS by h2 but leaves F unchanged.

Use this to keep numbers small.

Problem types you will face

Type 1 — one-way ANOVA, equal group sizes

Recipe: totals Ti​ → G → CT → ∑x2 → ST​, SB​, SW​ → ANOVA table → compare with Fα​ → comment.

Type 2 — one-way ANOVA, unequal group sizes

Use Ti2​/ni​ with each group’s own ni​. Nothing else changes.

Type 4 — two-way ANOVA (blocks × treatments)

Recognise it: a table where rows are blocks (soil, workers, days) and columns are treatments (varieties, machines), with one value per cell, and the question asks about both factors.

Recipe: row totals, column totals, CT, ST​, SR​, SC​, SE​ by subtraction, then two F ratios, each compared with its own critical value.

Type 5 — F-test for two variances

Recipe: put the larger variance on top:

F=smaller s2larger s2​

with df (numerator n−1, denominator n−1). For a two-tailed test at α, compare with Fα/2​.

Extra practice

Extra practice (try first, then open for answers)

E1. Groups: (8, 10, 12), (12, 14, 13), (6, 7, 8). One-way ANOVA at 5% (F0.05​(2,6)=5.14).

Answer: G=90, CT=900, ST​=66, SB​=54, SW​=12, so F=27/2=13.5>5.14. Reject.

E2. With k=4 groups and N=20, what are the df of the F ratio?

Answer: (3, 16).

E3. In a two-way table with 5 blocks and 4 treatments, what are the error df?

Answer: (5−1)(4−1)=12.

E4. Every observation is divided by 10. What happens to SS and to F?

Answer: every SS is divided by 100, and F is unchanged.

E5. s12​=80.9 with n1​=7 and s22​=19.1 with n2​=8. Is there a difference in variation at 10% (F0.05​(6,7)=3.87)?

Answer: F=4.23>3.87, so reject: the variances differ.
