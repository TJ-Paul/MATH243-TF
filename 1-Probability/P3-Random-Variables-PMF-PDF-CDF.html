P3 · Random Variables, PMF, PDF and CDF

Why this matters for the TF

A “density function” question appears almost every year: 2020-21 Q6(b) (find k for k(3−x)2), 2021-22 Q6(c) (build a distribution and F(x)), 2022-23 Q1(c) (CDF of an exponential density) and 2023-24 Q2(a) (probability using both the CDF and the pdf).

The last two are solved in TF 2022-23 Solutions and TF 2023-24 Solutions.

You need three skills: ==find k, compute a probability as a sum or an area, and move between f(x) and F(x)==.

Core tools (memorize)

Memorize

Discrete X (counts)

Use ∑=1 to find k.

f(x)=P(X=x)≥0 x∑​f(x)=1

Continuous X (measurements)

Use the total area =1 to find k.

∫−∞∞​f(x)dx=1

Probability is area

P(a<X<b)=∫ab​f(x)dx

For continuous X, P(X=a)=0, so < and ≤ give the same answer.

CDF (definition)

Discrete: add f(t) for t≤x (a step function).

F(x)=P(X≤x)

CDF from a pdf

F(x)=∫−∞x​f(t)dt

pdf from a CDF

f(x)=F′(x)

Probability from a CDF

P(a<X≤b)=F(b)−F(a)

Problem types you will face

Type 1 — Find k in a pmf

How to recognise it: a table or formula with an unknown constant, for discrete values.

Recipe:

  1. Add all probabilities.
  2. Set the sum to 1 and solve for k.
  3. Add the required values.

Practice 1.1

Given. f(x)=k(x2+1) for x=0,1,2,3. Find k and P(X≥2).

Step 1 — Sum to 1.

k(1+2+5+10)=18k=1 k=181​

Step 2 — Add the required values.

P(X≥2)=f(2)+f(3)=185+10​=65​

Answer. k=1/18, P(X≥2)=5/6.

Practice 1.2

Given. f(x)=kx for x=1,2,3,4. Find k and P(1<X≤3).

Step 1 — Sum to 1.

k(1+2+3+4)=10k=1 k=101​

Step 2 — Add the required values.

P(1<X≤3)=f(2)+f(3)=102+3​=0.5

Answer. k=1/10, P=0.5.

Type 2 — Find k in a pdf, then probabilities

How to recognise it: ”f(x)=k⋅(something) on an interval, determine k … find the probability that …“.

Recipe:

  1. Integrate over the whole range and set equal to 1.
  2. For each probability, integrate over the required interval.
  3. For “magnitude exceeds c”, use the complement:
P(∣X∣>c)=1−P(−c≤X≤c)

Past TF 2020-21 Q6(b) (10 marks) — measurement error

Given. f(x)=k(3−x)2 for −1≤x≤1, and 0 elsewhere.

Step 1 — (i) Find k.

∫−11​k(3−x)2dx​=k[−3(3−x)3​]−11​=k⋅343−23​=356k​=1​ k=563​

Step 2 — (ii) Error less than 21​, i.e. between −1 and 21​.

P(X<21​)​=563​⋅343−2.53​=5664−15.625​=5648.375​=0.8638​

Step 3 — (iii) Magnitude exceeds 0.8, i.e. ∣X∣>0.8. First find the middle part.

P(∣X∣≤0.8)​=563​⋅33.83−2.23​=5654.872−10.648​=5644.224​=0.7897​ P(∣X∣>0.8)=1−0.7897=0.2103

Answer. (i) k=3/56, (ii) 0.8638, (iii) 0.2103.

Practice 2.1

Given. f(x)=kx(2−x) for 0<x<2. Find k, P(X>1.5) and P(0.5<X<1).

Step 1 — Find k.

∫02​(2x−x2)dx=[x2−3x3​]02​=4−38​=34​ k=43​

Step 2 — P(X>1.5).

P(X>1.5)=43​[x2−3x3​]1.52​=43​(34​−1.125)=0.15625

Step 3 — P(0.5<X<1).

P(0.5<X<1)=43​[x2−3x3​]0.51​=43​(32​−245​)=0.34375

Answer. k=3/4, 0.15625, 0.34375.

Practice 2.2

Given. f(x)=ke−2x for x>0. Find k and P(X>1).

Step 1 — Find k.

∫0∞​ke−2xdx=2k​=1 k=2

Step 2 — P(X>1).

P(X>1)=∫1∞​2e−2xdx=e−2=0.1353

Answer. k=2, P(X>1)=0.1353.

Type 3 — CDF from a pdf (and checking with both)

How to recognise it: “find the cumulative distribution function”, “use F(x) to find …“.

Recipe:

  1. Integrate f from the left end of the support up to x.
  2. Write F piecewise, with a 0 line and a 1 line.
  3. Then P(a<X<b)=F(b)−F(a).

Practice 3.1

Given. f(x)=2x​ for 0<x<2. Find F(x) and P(0.5<X<1.5).

Step 1 — Integrate up to x. For 0<x<2,

F(x)=∫0x​2t​dt=4x2​

Step 2 — Write F piecewise.

F(x)=⎩⎨⎧​0,x2/4,1,​x≤00<x<2x≥2​

Step 3 — Probability from the CDF.

P(0.5<X<1.5)=F(1.5)−F(0.5)=42.25−0.25​=0.5

Step 4 — Check with the pdf.

∫0.51.5​2x​dx=0.5 ✓

Answer. F(x)=x2/4 on (0,2); P=0.5.

Practice 3.2 — piecewise (triangular) density

Given. f(x)=x for 0<x<1, f(x)=2−x for 1≤x<2, 0 elsewhere. Find F(x) and P(0.5<X<1.5).

Step 1 — First piece, 0<x<1.

F(x)=∫0x​tdt=2x2​

So F(1)=21​.

Step 2 — Second piece, 1≤x<2. Start from F(1).

F(x)=21​+∫1x​(2−t)dt=1−2(2−x)2​

Step 3 — Write F piecewise.

F(x)=⎩⎨⎧​0,x2/2,1−(2−x)2/2,1,​x≤00<x<11≤x<2x≥2​

Step 4 — Probability.

P(0.5<X<1.5)=F(1.5)−F(0.5)=0.875−0.125=0.75

Answer. P=0.75.

Piecewise densities

When f changes formula at x=1, the CDF in the second piece must start from F(1), not from 0.

Type 4 — Given the CDF: probabilities and the pdf

How to recognise it: a CDF like F(x)=1−e−λx is given (2023-24 Q2(a)), or a pdf like β1​e−x/β is given and you must find F (2022-23 Q1(c)).

Recipe:

  1. Convert units first.
  2. Use P(X≤a)=F(a) and P(X>a)=1−F(a).
  3. Differentiate F to get f, and confirm the same probability by integrating f.

Practice 4.1 (same shape as 2023-24 Q2(a))

Given. The waiting time in hours until a server receives a request has CDF F(x)=1−e−3x for x≥0. Find the probability of waiting less than 20 minutes, using both the CDF and the pdf.

Step 1 — Convert units. 20 minutes =31​ hour.

Step 2 — Using the CDF.

P(X<31​)=F(31​)=1−e−1=0.6321

Step 3 — Using the pdf. Differentiate:

f(x)=F′(x)=3e−3x ∫01/3​3e−3xdx=[−e−3x]01/3​=1−e−1=0.6321

Answer. P=0.6321 by both methods.

Practice 4.2 (same shape as 2022-23 Q1(c))

Given. The lifetime (hours) of a component has f(x)=501​e−x/50 for x≥0. Find (i) the CDF, (ii) P(lasts more than 100 hours), (iii) P(fails between 20 and 60 hours), (iv) the median lifetime.

Step 1 — (i) CDF. For x≥0 (and F=0 for x<0):

F(x)=∫0x​501​e−t/50dt=1−e−x/50

Step 2 — (ii) More than 100 hours.

P(X>100)=1−F(100)=e−2=0.1353

Step 3 — (iii) Between 20 and 60 hours.

P(20<X<60)​=F(60)−F(20)=e−0.4−e−1.2=0.6703−0.3012=0.3691​

Step 4 — (iv) Median. Solve F(m)=0.5.

e−m/50=0.5 m=50ln2=34.66 hours

Answer. (i) 1−e−x/50, (ii) 0.1353, (iii) 0.3691, (iv) 34.66 hours.

Units

If X is in hours and the question says minutes, convert first (12 minutes = 0.2 hour).

Type 5 — Distribution of a count from sampling without replacement, and its F(x)

How to recognise it: “a lot of N items contains k defective; n are taken; find the probability distribution of X = number defective, and F(x)“.

Recipe:

  1. For each possible x, use
f(x)=(nN​)(xk​)(n−xN−k​)​
  1. Make a table.
  2. Build the step CDF by running totals.

Past TF 2021-22 Q6(c) (15 marks) — cars with paint blemishes

Given. 5 cars, 2 blemished; the agency receives 3. X = number of blemished cars received, X=0,1,2.

Step 1 — Total samples.

(35​)=10

Step 2 — The pmf.

f(x)=10(x2​)(3−x3​)​ f(0)=101​ f(1)=106​ f(2)=103​
x012
f(x)1/106/103/10

Step 3 — The CDF (running totals).

F(x)=⎩⎨⎧​0,1/10,7/10,1,​x<00≤x<11≤x<2x≥2​

Answer. The table and F(x) above.

Practice 5.1 — markers

Given. A lot of 7 markers has 3 defective. A buyer takes 3 at random. Find the distribution of X = number of defective markers and F(x).

Step 1 — Total samples.

(37​)=35

Step 2 — The pmf. For x=0,1,2,3:

f(x)=35(x3​)(3−x4​)​
x0123
f(x)4/3518/3512/351/35

Step 3 — The CDF.

F(x)=⎩⎨⎧​0,4/35,22/35,34/35,1,​x<00≤x<11≤x<22≤x<3x≥3​

Answer. The table and F(x) above.

Practice 5.2 — coin tosses

Given. A coin is tossed 3 times; X = number of heads.

Step 1 — The pmf.

f(0)=81​ f(1)=83​ f(2)=83​ f(3)=81​

Answer. F jumps to 81​,84​,87​,1 at x=0,1,2,3.

Writing a discrete CDF

Use "≤" on the left and "<" on the right of each interval.

Always include the first line (F=0) and the last line (F=1).
