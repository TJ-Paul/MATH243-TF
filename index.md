# MATH 243 — Probability and Statistics: Term Final Guide

Welcome. This vault is a complete, exam-focused course for the MATH 243 (L-2/T-2, CSE) term final. It was built from the course lecture slides in the `resources` folder and from a careful study of the last five term finals (2019-20 to 2023-24). The goal is simple: by the time you finish these notes you should be able to solve **any** question from those five papers on your own, and the solutions of the two newest papers are kept for the very end as a final check.

> [!important] How this guide is organised
> The notes follow the order of the course: ==probability first, then statistics==, and they are ==problem-first==. Every topic note has the same shape: which past term-final questions use the topic, one box of core formulas to memorize, the theory the term final has actually asked (written as ready-to-copy exam answers, and nothing more), and then the main part, **"Problem types you will face"**, where each type of question is explained in a few lines and then solved through real past questions (2019-20, 2020-21, 2021-22) and fresh practice problems in the same style. Each note ends with extra practice and answers. The 2022-23 and 2023-24 papers are solved separately in the last folder so that you can use them as mock exams.

## Part 1 — Probability

Start here. Probability is the language of the whole course, and Section A of the MATH 243 paper is mostly probability.

1. [[P1 Probability Basics and Counting]] — sample spaces, events, counting, the addition rule, odds.
2. [[P2 Conditional Probability Independence and Bayes]] — the multiplication law, independence, total probability and ==Bayes' theorem== (asked almost every year).
3. [[P3 Random Variables PMF PDF CDF]] — discrete and continuous random variables, finding $k$, cumulative distribution functions.
4. [[P4 Joint Marginal and Conditional Distributions]] — joint tables and joint densities, marginals, conditionals, independence.
5. [[P5 Expectation Variance Covariance and Chebyshev]] — mean, variance, covariance, rules for $aX+b$, Chebyshev's theorem.
6. [[P6 Bernoulli Binomial Multinomial Hypergeometric Geometric]] — the counting distributions.
7. [[P7 Poisson Distribution and Poisson Process]] — Poisson, its derivation, and the Poisson approximation.
8. [[P8 Normal Distribution]] — the normal curve, the $Z$ table, and the normal approximation.
9. [[P9 Uniform Exponential and Other Continuous Distributions]] — exponential waiting times and the memoryless property.

## Part 2 — Statistics

1. [[S1 Introduction to Statistics Data and Sampling]] — what statistics is, types of data, sources in Bangladesh, sampling methods.
2. [[S2 Frequency Distributions and Graphs]] — Sturges' rule, histograms, ogives, stem-and-leaf plots, scatter and box plots.
3. [[S3 Measures of Central Tendency]] — mean, median, mode, quartiles and percentiles.
4. [[S4 Measures of Dispersion]] — variance, standard deviation, coefficient of variation, corrected values.
5. [[S5 Moments Skewness and Kurtosis]] — moments, Pearson, Bowley and Kelly skewness, $\beta_1,\beta_2,\gamma_1,\gamma_2$.
6. [[S6 Correlation and Regression]] — Pearson and rank correlation, regression lines, $R^2$, second-degree fitting.
7. [[S7 Sampling Distributions CLT and Confidence Intervals]] — the central limit theorem and confidence intervals.
8. [[S8 Hypothesis Testing Concepts and z t Tests]] — all testing concepts plus z, t and proportion tests.
9. [[S9 Chi-Square Tests]] — goodness of fit and contingency tables.
10. [[S10 ANOVA One-Way and Two-Way]] — analysis of variance.

## Part 3 — Exam toolkit

[[Formula Sheet]] collects every formula you need on one page. [[TF Question Map]] lists every question from the five papers with the note that teaches it. [[Exam Answer Templates and Strategy]] shows how to choose a method quickly and how to lay out answers for full marks.

## Part 4 — Term final solutions (read last)

[[TF 2023-24 Solutions]] and [[TF 2022-23 Solutions]] contain complete solutions to every part of both MATH 243 papers. Try each paper under timed conditions first, and only then compare.

## How to read the highlights

Throughout the notes, a red **Important / Memorize** box contains a formula or definition you must know exactly. A green **Tip** box gives an exam shortcut. A yellow **Warning** box points out a common mistake. A **Past TF** example box contains a real past question solved in full, and ==highlighted text== marks phrases worth remembering word for word. Each note ends with a collapsed self-check; click it open only after you have tried the questions.

> [!tip] Suggested schedule
> Spend roughly 60% of your time on Part 1 and Part 2, working every past example by hand, 20% on practising past papers from the [[TF Question Map]], and the last 20% on sitting the 2022-23 and 2023-24 papers as timed mock exams.

The original lecture slides and the past-paper PDF are in the `resources` folder if you want to look at the source material.
