# TF Question Map (2019-20 to 2023-24)

This page maps every probability and statistics question from the last five term finals to the note where the concept is taught and, for the older papers, where the question is solved in full. Use it in two ways. Before studying a topic, look at how it has been asked so you know what to focus on. After studying a topic, come back here and make sure you can solve every question listed for it without looking.

> [!important] Read this first: what the papers look like
> The 2022-23 and 2023-24 papers are **MATH 243 (Probability and Statistics)**. Both sections (A and B) are probability and statistics, 8 questions in total, and you answer any three from each section. The 2019-20, 2020-21 and 2021-22 papers are **MATH 245 (Complex Variable and Statistics)**. In those papers Section A was complex variables, which is ==not in your syllabus==, so only their Section B (Q5 to Q8) matters for you. That is why the newest two papers are solved separately in [[TF 2023-24 Solutions]] and [[TF 2022-23 Solutions]], while the Section B questions of the older three papers are solved inside the topic notes as worked "Past TF" examples.

## What keeps coming back

Looking across all five papers, some question types appear almost every year, so they deserve the most practice. ==Bayes' theorem and total probability== appears in four of the five papers (truth serum, machines, party supporters, ball transfer). ==Binomial, Poisson and normal probability calculations== appear in every paper, usually with a real-life story, so you must be fast at choosing the right distribution and reading the normal table. ==Joint and continuous densities== (finding $k$, marginals, conditional density, expected values) appear in every MATH 243 paper and in 2020-21.

On the statistics side, ==moments about an assumed mean with $\beta_1,\beta_2$ or $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$== appears in 2019-20, 2022-23 and 2023-24, and two of those are the exact examples from the lecture slides. ==Regression lines and correlation== (including rank correlation with ties and a second-degree polynomial fit) appear every single year. Hypothesis testing is guaranteed: a ==z-test or t-test for a mean== every year, plus a ==chi-square contingency test== and a ==one-way ANOVA== in both MATH 243 papers. Finally, the newest papers have started asking short theory questions (CLT and its use in computer science, stratified sampling, stem-and-leaf plots, sources of statistical data in Bangladesh, differences between testing concepts), so do not skip the theory paragraphs in [[S1 Introduction to Statistics Data and Sampling]], [[S2 Frequency Distributions and Graphs]], [[S7 Sampling Distributions CLT and Confidence Intervals]] and [[S8 Hypothesis Testing Concepts and z t Tests]].

> [!tip] Lecture examples come back word for word
> 2023-24 Q4(c) (nitrogen dose and rice yield), 2023-24 Q8(a) (job performance vs education), 2022-23 Q4(b) (Bangla and English marks) and 2022-23 Q6(c) and 2019-20 Q5(b) (moments) are copied directly from the lecture slides. Practising the lecture examples is therefore directly practising the exam.
