S7 · Sampling Distributions, CLT and Confidence Intervals

Core tools (memorize)

Memorize

E(Xˉ)=μ

Standard error of the sample mean:

SE(Xˉ)=n​σ​

Standardising Xˉ (normal for large n):

Z=σ/n​Xˉ−μ​≈N(0,1)

Theory the TF actually asks (2023-24 Q6b, keep in full)

Central Limit Theorem — statement

==If Xˉ is the mean of a random sample of size n taken from any population with mean μ and finite variance σ2, then as n becomes large the distribution of Xˉ approaches the normal distribution with mean μ and variance σ2/n:==

Z=σ/n​Xˉ−μ​ ⟶ N(0,1)as n→∞.

In practice the approximation is good when n≥30, whatever the shape of the population. If the population is itself normal, Xˉ is exactly normal for every n.

The theorem is important because it lets us use the normal table for sample means and totals even when we do not know the population’s distribution. That is the basis of large-sample z-tests and confidence intervals.

An algorithm to solve problems using the CLT

Step 1. Identify the population mean μ, population standard deviation σ (or the sample SD s for large n), and the sample size n.

Step 2. Check the conditions: random, independent observations and n≥30 (or a normal population).

Step 3. Compute the standard error SE=σ/n​. If the question is about a total ∑X, use mean nμ and SD σn​, or divide the total by n to turn it into a question about Xˉ.

Step 4. Convert the required value(s) of xˉ to z=(xˉ−μ)/SE.

Step 5. Read Φ(z) from the standard normal table and combine the areas: left tail Φ(z), right tail 1−Φ(z), between Φ(z2​)−Φ(z1​).

Step 6. State the answer in the words of the problem.

The same algorithm written as pseudo-code:

input mu, sigma, n, a, b
se = sigma / sqrt(n)
z1 = (a - mu) / se ;  z2 = (b - mu) / se
prob = Phi(z2) - Phi(z1)
output prob

Applications of the CLT in Computer Science

Performance measurement and benchmarking. The average response time, latency or execution time over many runs is approximately normal. So we can report a mean with a confidence interval and test whether a new algorithm is really faster.

A/B testing of software and websites. Click-through rates and conversion averages from many users are compared with z-tests that rely on the CLT.

Monte Carlo simulation and randomized algorithms. The average of many random trials, as in estimating π or an integral, is approximately normal. This lets us compute the error of the estimate and the number of trials needed.

Networking and queueing. The total traffic, packet delay or load from many independent users is approximately normal. This is used for capacity planning and setting buffer sizes.

Machine learning and data science. Averaged errors, cross-validation scores, bootstrap estimates and mini-batch gradients behave approximately normally, which justifies confidence intervals for model accuracy.

Quality control and reliability. Average defect counts or failure times from samples are monitored with control charts based on normal limits.

Point vs interval estimate, in one line each

A point estimate is a single value used to estimate a parameter (xˉ for μ).

An interval estimate (confidence interval) is a range that contains the parameter with a stated confidence. 95% means that 95% of such intervals built from repeated samples would contain μ.

Problem types you will face

Type 1 — probability about a sample mean (CLT)

Recognise it: “A random sample of n is taken… find the probability that the sample mean is…“.

Recipe: follow the CLT algorithm above.

Practice 1 (TF style): bulb lifetimes

Bulb life has mean 800 h and SD 40 h. Find the probability that the mean life of a random sample of 16 bulbs is less than 775 h.

Step 1 — standard error.

SE=16​40​=10

Step 2 — standardise.

z=10775−800​=−2.5

Step 3 — probability.

P(Xˉ<775)=Φ(−2.5)=1−0.9938=0.0062

Answer. 0.0062. (The population of lifetimes is assumed normal here, since n=16<30.)

Practice 2 (TF style): mean between two values

A population has μ=50 and σ=12. For n=36, find P(48<Xˉ<53).

Step 1 — standard error.

SE=612​=2

Step 2 — standardise both ends.

z1​=248−50​=−1

z2​=253−50​=1.5

Step 3 — probability.

P &= \Phi(1.5)-\Phi(-1)\\ &= 0.9332-(1-0.8413)\\ &= 0.7745 \end{aligned}$$ **Answer.** **0.7745.**

Practice 3 (TF style): skewed population, large sample

Waiting times are exponential with mean 4 minutes, so σ=4. For 64 customers, find P(Xˉ>4.5).

Step 1 — check the CLT. The population is skewed, but n=64≥30, so the CLT applies.

Step 2 — standard error and z.

SE=84​=0.5

z=0.54.5−4​=1

Step 3 — probability.

P(Xˉ>4.5)=1−Φ(1)=1−0.8413=0.1587

Answer. 0.1587.

Practice 4 (TF style): a total (elevator problem)

Passenger weights have μ=70 kg and σ=14 kg. A lift carries 49 passengers and has a limit of 3600 kg. Find the probability that the limit is exceeded.

Step 1 — turn the total into a mean. A total above 3600 means a mean above

493600​=73.47

Step 2 — standard error and z.

SE=714​=2

z=273.47−70​=1.73

Step 3 — probability.

P=1−Φ(1.73)=1−0.9582=0.0418

Answer. 0.0418.

Type 2 — confidence interval for a mean

Recognise it: “Compute a 95% / 99% confidence interval for the population mean.”

Recipe: decide between z (σ known or n≥30) and t (small n, σ unknown). Compute the margin E, and write xˉ−E≤μ≤xˉ+E with a one-line interpretation.

Practice 5 (TF style): σ known, 95%

n=36, xˉ=2.6, σ=0.3.

Step 1 — margin of error.

E=1.96×60.3​=0.098

Step 2 — interval.

2.6−0.098≤μ≤2.6+0.098

Answer. 95% CI: ==2.502≤μ≤2.698==.

Practice 6 (TF style): large sample, 99%

n=100, xˉ=42, s=8.

Step 1 — margin of error.

E=2.58×108​=2.064

Step 2 — interval.

42−2.064≤μ≤42+2.064

Answer. 99% CI: ==39.94≤μ≤44.06==. We are 99% confident that the population mean lies in this range.

Practice 7 (TF style): small sample from raw data, t-interval

Ten measurements: 9.8, 10.2, 10.4, 9.8, 10.0, 10.2, 9.6, 10.1, 10.3, 9.9. Find a 95% CI.

Step 1 — totals and mean.

∑x=100.3∑x2=1006.59

xˉ=10.03

Step 2 — sample SD.

s^2 &= \frac{1006.59-10(10.03)^2}{9}\\ &= \frac{0.581}{9}\\ &= 0.06456 \end{aligned}$$ $$s=0.2541$$ **Step 3 — critical value.** df = 9, so $$t_{0.025,9}=2.262$$ **Step 4 — margin of error.** $$E=2.262\times\frac{0.2541}{\sqrt{10}}=0.182$$ **Answer.** 95% CI: ==$9.848\le\mu\le10.212$==.

Practice 8 (TF style): small sample, summary data, 99%

n=16, xˉ=27.9, s=3.23.

Step 1 — critical value. df = 15, so

t0.005,15​=2.947

Step 2 — margin of error.

E=2.947×43.23​=2.380

Answer. 99% CI: ==25.52≤μ≤30.28==.

Type 3 — sample size (occasionally asked)

Practice 9 (TF style)

How large a sample is needed to estimate μ within 0.05 with 95% confidence if σ=0.3?

Step 1 — formula.

n=(0.051.96×0.3​)2=138.3

Step 2 — round up.

Answer. n=139.

Extra practice

Extra practice (try first, then open for answers)

E1. μ=50, σ=8, n=25. Find P(Xˉ>52).

Answer: z=2/1.6=1.25, so P=0.1056.

E2. n=64, xˉ=120, s=16. Find the 95% CI.

Answer: 120±1.96(2), which is 116.08 to 123.92.

E3. n=9, xˉ=20, s=3. Find the 95% CI (t0.025,8​=2.306).

Answer: 20±2.306, which is 17.69 to 22.31.

E4. Why can a z-interval be used for n=100 even when the population is skewed?

Answer: by the CLT, Xˉ is approximately normal for large n.

E5. How does the width of a CI change if n is multiplied by 4?

Answer: it halves, because the width is proportional to 1/n​.
