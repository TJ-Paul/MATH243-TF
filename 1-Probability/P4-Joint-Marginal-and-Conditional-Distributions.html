P4 · Joint, Marginal and Conditional Distributions

Why this matters for the TF

Joint distributions were asked in 2020-21 Q5(c) (joint density k(x2+y2)), 2022-23 Q2(a) (density 10xy2 on a triangle: marginals, conditional, conditional probability) and 2023-24 Q2(b) (20 marks: joint pmf from drawing pens, then P[(X,Y)∈A], marginals, variance and covariance).

The last two are solved in TF 2022-23 Solutions and TF 2023-24 Solutions.

Every one of those shapes is practised below.

Core tools (memorize)

Memorize

Joint pmf (discrete)

f(x,y)=P(X=x, Y=y) x∑​y∑​f(x,y)=1

P[(X,Y)∈A] is the sum of the cells in A.

Joint pdf (continuous)

Use the total =1 to find k.

∬f(x,y)dxdy=1 P[(X,Y)∈A]=∬A​f(x,y)dxdy

Marginals

Discrete: row and column totals. Continuous:

g(x)=∫f(x,y)dy h(y)=∫f(x,y)dx

Conditional

f(y∣x)=g(x)f(x,y)​ f(x∣y)=h(y)f(x,y)​ P(a<Y<b∣X=x)=∫ab​f(y∣x)dy

Independence

X and Y are independent if, for every (x,y),

f(x,y)=g(x)h(y)

If the support is not a rectangle (for example 0<x<y<1), X and Y are not independent.

Drawing objects of three colours (sample of size n)

Valid for x+y≤n.

f(x,y)=(nN​)(xN1​​)(yN2​​)(n−x−yN3​​)​

Problem types you will face

Type 1 — Joint pmf from drawing objects (the 2023-24 Q2(b) shape)

How to recognise it: “a bag contains … of three colours, n are selected; X = number of colour 1, Y = number of colour 2”.

Recipe:

  1. List all pairs with x+y≤n.
  2. Compute each cell with the three-colour formula.
  3. Fill the table with row and column totals (the marginals) and check it adds to 1.
  4. Answer P[(X,Y)∈A] by adding cells.
  5. Find E(X), E(X2) and Var(X) from g(x); E(XY) from the table; then Cov=E(XY)−E(X)E(Y).

Practice 1.1 (full 2023-24 Q2(b) shape)

Given. A box has 4 red, 3 green and 2 white balls. Two balls are drawn at random. X = number of red, Y = number of green. (i) Find the joint distribution and plot it. (ii) Find P[(X,Y)∈A], A={(x,y):x+y≤1}. (iii) Marginals. (iv) Var(X). (v) Cov(X,Y).

Step 1 — (i) Total samples.

(29​)=36

Step 2 — (i) Two sample cells.

f(1,1)=36(14​)(13​)(02​)​=3612​ f(0,0)=36(22​)​=361​

All cells:

f(x,y)y=0y=1y=2g(x)
x=01/366/363/3610/36

Plot. Draw the x and y axes on the floor and mark the six points (0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0). Draw a vertical stick at each point with height f(x,y). The tallest stick is at (1,1), height 31​.

Step 3 — (ii) Add the cells with x+y≤1: (0,0),(0,1),(1,0).

P[(X,Y)∈A]=361+6+8​=3615​=125​

Step 4 — (iii) Marginals (row and column totals).

g(0)=185​,g(1)=95​,g(2)=61​ h(0)=125​,h(1)=21​,h(2)=121​

Step 5 — (iv) Variance of X.

E(X)=95​+2⋅61​=98​ E(X2)=95​+4⋅61​=911​ Var(X)=911​−8164​=8135​≈0.4321

Step 6 — (v) Covariance. Only the (1,1) cell has xy=0.

E(XY)=1⋅1⋅3612​=31​ E(Y)=21​+2⋅121​=32​ Cov(X,Y)​=E(XY)−E(X)E(Y)=31​−98​⋅32​=279−16​=−277​≈−0.2593​

The negative covariance makes sense: more red balls leave less room for green ones.

Common mistakes

Forgetting the third-colour factor (n−x−yN3​​).

Including impossible cells such as (1,2) when only 2 items are drawn.

Always check the table totals 1.

Type 2 — Joint pmf given by a formula

How to recognise it: ”f(x,y)=k(…) for x=…, y=…“.

Recipe:

  1. Add over all cells to find k.
  2. Find the marginals by summing.
  3. Conditional = cell ÷ marginal.
  4. Check independence by comparing f with g⋅h.

Practice 2.1

Given. f(x,y)=k(x+y) for x=0,1,2 and y=0,1,2,3. Find (i) k, (ii) the marginals, (iii) P(X≤1,Y≥2), (iv) f(y∣x=1), (v) Cov(X,Y).

Step 1 — (i) Find k.

∑∑(x+y)=4(0+1+2)+3(0+1+2+3)=12+18=30 k=301​

Step 2 — (ii) Marginal of X.

g(x)=y=0∑3​30x+y​=304x+6​ g(0)=51​,g(1)=31​,g(2)=157​

Step 3 — (ii) Marginal of Y.

h(y)=x=0∑2​30x+y​=303+3y​ h(0)=101​,h(1)=51​,h(2)=103​,h(3)=52​

Step 4 — (iii) Add cells (0,2),(0,3),(1,2),(1,3).

P(X≤1, Y≥2)=302+3+3+4​=3012​=52​

Step 5 — (iv) Conditional. For y=0,1,2,3:

f(y∣1)=1/3(1+y)/30​=101+y​

Step 6 — (v) Covariance.

E(X)=31​+2⋅157​=1519​ E(Y)=51​+106​+56​=2 E(XY)=∑∑xy30x+y​=512​ Cov(X,Y)=512​−1519​⋅2=1536−38​=−152​

Answer. k=1/30, (iii) 2/5, (v) −2/15.

Since Cov=0, X and Y are not independent (also f(0,0)=0=g(0)h(0)).

Type 3 — Joint pdf on a rectangle: find k and rectangle probabilities

How to recognise it: ”f(x,y)=k(…) for a≤x<b, c≤y<d“.

Recipe:

  1. Integrate over y first (treat x as a constant), then over x.
  2. Set the total to 1 to find k.
  3. For each probability, use the same integral with the new limits.
  4. Translate words into limits carefully (“underfilled” = below the target).

Past TF 2020-21 Q5(c) (10 marks) — tyre pressures

Given. f(x,y)=k(x2+y2) for 30≤x<50, 30≤y<50.

Step 1 — (i) Inner integral. For fixed x:

∫3050​(x2+y2)dy=20x2+3503−303​=20x2+398000​

Step 2 — (i) Outer integral.

∫3050​(20x2+398000​)dx=20⋅398000​+20⋅398000​=33,920,000​ k=3,920,0003​≈7.653×10−7

Step 3 — (ii) P(30≤X≤40, 40≤Y<50). For fixed x:

∫4050​(x2+y2)dy=10x2+361000​ ∫3040​(10x2+361000​)dx=3370000+610000​=3980000​ P=3,920,000980000​=0.25

Step 4 — (iii) Both underfilled (30≤X<40, 30≤Y<40).

∫3040​∫3040​(x2+y2)dydx=3370000​+3370000​=3740000​ P=3,920,000740000​=19637​≈0.1888

Answer. (i) k=3/3,920,000, (ii) 0.25, (iii) 0.1888.

Practice 3.1

Given. f(x,y)=k(x+2y) for 0<x<1, 0<y<1. Find k, P(X<21​,Y<21​), the marginals, and check independence.

Step 1 — Find k.

∫01​∫01​(x+2y)dydx=∫01​(x+1)dx=23​ k=32​

Step 2 — Rectangle probability.

P(X<21​, Y<21​)​=32​∫01/2​∫01/2​(x+2y)dydx=32​∫01/2​(2x​+41​)dx=32​⋅163​=81​​

Step 3 — Marginals.

g(x)=32​∫01​(x+2y)dy=32​(x+1) h(y)=32​∫01​(x+2y)dx=32​(21​+2y)

Step 4 — Independence. The product g(x)h(y) is not 32​(x+2y).

Answer. k=2/3, P=1/8, not independent.

Practice 3.2 — an independent case

Given. f(x,y)=kxy for 0<x<1, 0<y<2. Find k and show X, Y are independent.

Step 1 — Find k.

k∫01​xdx∫02​ydy=k⋅21​⋅2=k=1

Step 2 — Marginals.

g(x)=∫02​xydy=2x h(y)=∫01​xydx=2y​

Step 3 — Check.

g(x)h(y)=2x⋅2y​=xy=f(x,y)

Answer. k=1, and X, Y are independent.

Type 4 — Joint pdf on a triangle: marginals, conditional, conditional probability

How to recognise it: support like 0<x<y<1 (the 2022-23 Q2(a) shape).

Recipe:

  1. Draw the region.
  2. For g(x), integrate over y from the line to the edge.
  3. For h(y), integrate over x.
  4. f(y∣x)=f/g.
  5. Substitute the given x and integrate over the required y-range (keeping inside the support).

Practice 4.1

Given. f(x,y)=8xy for 0<y<x<1. Find the marginals, f(y∣x) and P(Y<41​∣X=21​).

Step 1 — Limits. For fixed x, y runs from 0 to x. For fixed y, x runs from y to 1.

Step 2 — Marginal of X (0<x<1).

g(x)=∫0x​8xydy=4x3

Step 3 — Marginal of Y (0<y<1).

h(y)=∫y1​8xydx=4y(1−y2)

Step 4 — Conditional (0<y<x).

f(y∣x)=4x38xy​=x22y​

Step 5 — Conditional probability. At x=21​, f(y∣21​)=8y on 0<y<21​.

P(Y<41​∣X=21​)=∫01/4​8ydy=41​

Answer. g(x)=4x3, h(y)=4y(1−y2), f(y∣x)=2y/x2, P=1/4.

Practice 4.2

Given. f(x,y)=6x for 0<x<y<1. Find g(x), h(y), f(x∣y), P(X<41​∣Y=21​) and P(X+Y<1).

Step 1 — Marginal of X (0<x<1).

g(x)=∫x1​6xdy=6x(1−x)

Step 2 — Marginal of Y (0<y<1).

h(y)=∫0y​6xdx=3y2

Step 3 — Conditional (0<x<y).

f(x∣y)=3y26x​=y22x​

Step 4 — Conditional probability. At y=21​, f(x∣21​)=8x on 0<x<21​.

P(X<41​∣Y=21​)=∫01/4​8xdx=41​

Step 5 — P(X+Y<1). Inside the triangle, x runs from 0 to 21​ and y from x to 1−x.

P(X+Y<1)​=∫01/2​∫x1−x​6xdydx=∫01/2​6x(1−2x)dx=6(81​−121​)=41​​

Answer. g=6x(1−x), h=3y2, f(x∣y)=2x/y2, 1/4, 1/4.

Limits on a triangle

Write the limits next to the sketch before integrating.

Marginals of triangle densities always have the variable in the limits, so they look like 6x(1−x) or 310x​(1−x3), never a constant.

Extra practice

Answers (try first)

1. From 3 red, 2 blue, 1 green pen, 2 are chosen. X = red, Y = blue. Find f(1,1) and P(X=0).

Answer: f(1,1)=153⋅2​=52​; P(X=0)=15(23​)​=51​.

2. f(x,y)=cxy for x=1,2, y=1,2,3. Find c.

Answer: c(1+2)(1+2+3)=18c=1, so c=181​ (and X, Y are independent).

3. f(x,y)=4xy on the unit square. Find P(X<21​,Y>21​).

Answer: 41​⋅43​=163​.

4. f(x,y)=2 on 0<x<y<1. Find g(x) and f(y∣x).

Answer: g(x)=2(1−x); f(y∣x)=1−x1​, x<y<1.

5. Why can’t X, Y be independent on 0<x<y<1?

Answer: The support is not a rectangle.
