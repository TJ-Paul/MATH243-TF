S9 · Chi-Square Tests

Why this matters for the TF. Both MATH 243 papers had a chi-square contingency question: a 3×3 table on education and job performance (2023-24 Q8a, 15 marks, the lecture example) and a 2×2 table on a drug and typhoid (2022-23 Q8a). 2023-24 Q7(a) also asked a short note on goodness of fit.

These questions are completely mechanical, so they are safe marks. The two papers are solved in TF 2023-24 Solutions and TF 2022-23 Solutions.

Core tools (memorize)

Memorize

Chi-square statistic — use in all chi-square tests:

χ2=∑E(O−E)2​

Shortcut form (same value):

χ2=∑EO2​−N

Goodness of fit — use when asked whether counts follow a given distribution or ratio:

Ei​=Npi​

with df =k−1−m, where m is the number of parameters estimated from the data.

Contingency table, r×c — use when asked whether two attributes are associated:

Eij​=NRi​×Cj​​

with df =(r−1)(c−1).

2×2 table with Yates’ correction (cells a,b,c,d), df =1:

χ2=(a+b)(c+d)(a+c)(b+d)N(∣ad−bc∣−2N​)2​

Decision. The test is always right-tailed. Reject H0​ if χcal2​≥χα,df2​.

Critical values at 5%:

df 1: 3.84 · df 2: 5.99 · df 3: 7.81 · df 4: 9.49 · df 5: 11.07

Minimal theory

H0​ always says “no association” (independence) or “the data fit the stated distribution”.

The test needs random samples and every expected frequency at least 5; merge small classes otherwise.

A large χ2 means observed and expected frequencies disagree.

Common mistakes

Expected frequencies must be computed from the totals, never copied from the observed table.

Check that every row of E adds up to the row total.

Use frequencies, never percentages.

Problem types you will face

Type 1 — r×c contingency table (test of independence)

Recipe: write H0​/H1​. Compute every Eij​=Ri​Cj​/N (put them in brackets in the table). Make a table of (O−E)2/E, sum it, and compare with χα,(r−1)(c−1)2​.

Practice 1 (TF style): gender and first-choice major (4×2)

Given.

MajorMaleFemaleTotal
Mathematics122941
Biology271643
Physics151934
Chemistry91423
Total6378141

Hypothesis. H0​: gender and choice of major are independent. H1​: they are associated.

Level of significance. α=0.05.

Step 1 — expected frequencies.

E11​=14141×63​=18.32

E12​=14141×78​=22.68

The other rows follow in the same way: Biology 19.21 and 23.79, Physics 15.19 and 18.81, Chemistry 10.28 and 12.72.

Step 2 — chi-square table.

CellOE(O−E)2/E
Math, M1218.322.180
Math, F2922.681.761

Bio, F1623.792.549
Phys, M1515.190.002
Phys, F1918.810.002

Total1411419.94

Critical region. df =(4−1)(2−1)=3, so reject if χ2≥χ0.05,32​=7.81.

Calculated value. χ2=9.94.

Decision. 9.94>7.81, so reject H0​.

Comment. Choice of major is associated with gender. Males lean towards biology and females towards mathematics.

Practice 2 (TF style): immunization and mother's education (2×4)

Given.

IlliteratePrimarySecondaryHigherTotal
Immunized1501008070400
Not immunized40302010100
Total19013010080500

Hypothesis. H0​: the immunization rate does not depend on education.

Level of significance. α=0.05.

Step 1 — expected frequencies.

Row 1: 400×190/500=152, then 104, 80, 64.

Row 2: 38, 26, 20, 16.

Step 2 — chi-square.

χ2=1524​+10416​+0+6436​+384​+2616​+0+1636​

χ2=0.026+0.154+0+0.563+0.105+0.615+0+2.25=3.71

Critical region. df =(2−1)(4−1)=3; critical value 7.81.

Decision. 3.71<7.81, so do not reject H0​.

Comment. Immunization rates do not differ significantly across education levels.

Type 2 — 2×2 table (use Yates’ correction)

Recipe: label the cells a,b (row 1) and c,d (row 2), find the four margins, and use the shortcut formula. Compare with 3.84 at 5%.

If the question does not mention Yates, it is safest to show the corrected value and mention the uncorrected one.

Practice 3 (TF style): smoking and lung disease

Given.

DiseaseNo diseaseTotal
Smoker36 (a)14 (b)50
Non-smoker18 (c)32 (d)50
Total5446100

Hypothesis. H0​: lung disease is independent of smoking.

Level of significance. α=0.05; critical value χ0.05,12​=3.84.

Step 1 — cross-product difference.

ad−bc=36×32−14×18=1152−252=900

Step 2 — calculated value.

χ2=50×50×54×46100(900−50)2​=6,210,00072,250,000​=11.63

Decision. 11.63>3.84, so reject H0​.

Comment. Lung disease is associated with smoking. (Without Yates’ correction χ2=13.04, which gives the same decision.)

ad−bc=42×30−18×30=720

Practice 5 (TF style): gender and job type

Given. Male: 40 technical, 20 non-technical. Female: 25 technical, 35 non-technical. N=120.

Step 1 — cross-product difference.

ad−bc=40×35−20×25=900

Step 2 — calculated value.

χ2=60×60×65×55120(900−60)2​=6.58

Decision. 6.58>3.84, so reject H0​.

Comment. Job type depends on gender.

Type 3 — goodness of fit

Recipe: compute expected counts from the hypothesised probabilities (E=Np), then χ2. Use df =k−1, or fewer if parameters were estimated from the data.

Practice 6 (TF style): is the die fair?

120 throws give the faces 1 to 6 with frequencies 25, 17, 15, 23, 24, 16. Test at 5%.

Hypothesis. H0​: the die is fair, so pi​=1/6 and every Ei​=20.

Step 1 — chi-square table.

Face123456
O251715232416
E202020202020
(O−E)2/E1.250.451.250.450.800.80

Calculated value.

χ2=5.00

Critical region. df =6−1=5; critical value 11.07.

Decision. 5.00<11.07, so do not reject H0​.

Comment. The die can be regarded as fair.

Practice 7 (TF style): fit to a 9:3:3:1 ratio

Observed pea types: 315, 108, 101, 32 (total 556). Do they fit the 9:3:3:1 ratio, at 5%?

Step 1 — expected frequencies.

556×169​=312.75

556×163​=104.25 (twice)

556×161​=34.75

Step 2 — calculated value.

\chi^2 &= \frac{2.25^2}{312.75}+\frac{3.75^2}{104.25}+\frac{3.25^2}{104.25}+\frac{2.75^2}{34.75}\\ &= 0.016+0.135+0.101+0.218\\ &= 0.47 \end{aligned}$$ **Critical region.** df $=3$; critical value 7.81. **Decision.** $0.47<7.81$, so do not reject $H_0$. **Comment.** The data fit the 9:3:3:1 ratio very well.

Extra practice

Extra practice (try first, then open for answers)

E1. 200 births: 115 boys and 85 girls. Are boys and girls equally likely, at 5%?

Answer: E=100 each, χ2=2.25+2.25=4.5>3.84, so reject: they are not equally likely.

E2. 2×2 table with a=20, b=30, c=40, d=10. Test independence at 5% with Yates’ correction.

Answer: ∣ad−bc∣=1000, N=100, χ2=15.04>3.84, so reject.

E3. What is the df for a 3×4 contingency table?

Answer: (3−1)(4−1)=6.

E4. A cell has R=60, C=50, N=200. Find E.

Answer: 60×50/200=15.

E5. Why is the chi-square test always right-tailed?

Answer: because χ2≥0, and only large values show disagreement with H0​.
